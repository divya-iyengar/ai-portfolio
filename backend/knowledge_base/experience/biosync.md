# BioSync

## Role

Co-Founder
Georgia Tech Startup Launch Program
Senior Year Undergraduate / Summer Accelerator
January 2022 - August 2022

## Summary 

BioSync began as an interdisciplinary senior capstone project focused on identifying and solving real-world problems in the life sciences industry. Unike a traditional engineering capstone, the course emphasized entrepreneurship alongside technical development, requiring teams to validate customer needs before building a solution.

Our team conducted customer discovery, developed a business thesis, built a functional prototype, and refined our value proposition through continuous feedback from industry stakeholders. Following the conclusion of the capstone course, our team earned second place within the program and was invited into the Georgia Tech Startup Launch accelerator, where I continued as a co-founder.

The experience provided early exposure to product development, customer engagement, startup operations, and technical entrepreneurship. 

## Product 

BioSync was an IoT-enabled system designed to automate blood sample metadata tracking for clinical research workflows.

The objective was to improve traceability, reduce manual documentation errors, and strengthen compliance during FDA-regulated audits.

Rather than focusing solely on technology, our team approached the problem from both engineering and customer perspectives, ensuring the product addressed real operational pain points within laboratories and clinical research organizations. 

## Responsibilities

As a co-founder, I contributed across both technical and business development activities.

Technical responsibilities included: 
- Developing an IoT-enabled prototype for automated blood sample metadata tracking
- Iterating on the prototype based on user feedback 
- Supporting demonstrations of the product with prospective industry partners

Business responsibilities included: 
- Conducting customer discovery interviews with auditors, contract research organizations (CROs), central laboratories, and pharamaceutical companies
- Performing site visits to better understand existing laboratory workflows
- Identifying workflow inefficiencies and validating product-market fit
- Refining the team's value proposition based on customer feedback
- Contributing to investor presentations and pitch development during the Startup Launch accelerator

The combination of engineering development and customer engagement provided firsthand experience translating technical ideas into products that addressed measurable business problems.

## Outcomes 

The project achieved several significant milestones:
- Awarded Second Place within the Georgia Tech entrepreneurial capstone program
- Accepted into the Georgia Tech Startup Launch accelerator
- Advanced to the Georgia Tech InVenture Prize semifinals
- Demonstrated the product to RedCap to explore potential opportunities
- Completed customer validation through interviews and prototype evaluations with industry stakeholders

Although I transitioned to graduate school following the accelerator, the experience provided valuable insight into how engineering, business strategy, and customer needs intersect during early-stage product development.

## Lessons Learned

BioSync fundamentally changed how I think about engineering. Rather than beginning with a technical solution, I learned to start with customer problems, validate assumptions through interviews, and iteratively refine both the product and the business case.

Working directly with auditors, laboratory staff, CROs, and pharmaceutical stakeholders demonstrated that successful technology adoption depends not only on technical capability but also on workflow integration, usability, and clearly communicated business value.

Those lessons continue to influence how I approach engineering today by emphasizing customer understanding alongside technical execution.
