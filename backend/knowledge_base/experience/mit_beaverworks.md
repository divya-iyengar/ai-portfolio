# MIT BeaverWorks

## Role

Associate Instructor - Autonomous Racecar Course
MIT BeaverWorks Summer Institute
Summer 2019

## Summary 

During the summer of 2019, I served as one of five Associate Instructors for the MIT BeaverWorks Summer Institute Autonomous Racecar course. The program provided students with hands-on experience developing autonomous driving software on small-scale racecars, integrating perception, localization, and control algorithms on embedded robotic platforms.

As an instructor, my primary responsibility was helping student teams debug and integrate software throughout the course while reinforcing robotics concepts through practical implementation. This experience strengthened my ability to diagnose complex technical issues, communicate engineering concepts clearly, and support teams working under tight project timelines. 

## Responsibilities

Throughout the program, I supported approximately fifteen student teams as they progressed through increasingly complex autonomy labs.

My responsibilities included: 
- Managing and troubleshooting autonomous racecars equipped with NVIDIA Jetson TX2 embedded computers, Hokuyo LiDAR sensors, and onboard cameras
- Assisting students with software debugging during laboratory exercises and project milestones
- Helping teams understand and implement robotics concepts including localization, computer vision, color segmentation using OpenCV, and AR tag detection
- Supporting the integration of perception algorithms with autonomous vehicle behavior
- Guiding students through technical roadblocks while encouraging independent problem solving

Because every team encountered different software and hardware integration issues, the role required quickly diagnosing unfamiliar problems and adapting explanations to students with varying levels of programming and robotics experience.

 ## Lessons Learned

 Serving as an Associate Instructor reinforced that understanding a technical concept and teaching it effectively are two different skills. Working with many student teams required quickly identifying the root cause of software and hardware issues while tailoring explanations to each team's level of experience. 

 The experience also deepened my interest in autonomous systems by exposing me to the challenges of integrating perception, localization, and embedded computing into complete robotic platforms. These experiences later influenced my graduate research in robotics and ultimately my work in autonomous driving and applied AI.
